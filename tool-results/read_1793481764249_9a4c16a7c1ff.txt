    92→    92→# 4. M6B.1 — SOIL PROFILE AND SAMPLING INTELLIGENCE
    93→    93→
    94→    94→Build a comprehensive soil sampling and profile management domain.
    95→    95→
    96→    96→## 4.1 Soil profile representation
    97→    97→
    98→    98→Support multiple soil layers with explicit:
    99→    99→
   100→   100→- Top depth.
   101→   101→- Bottom depth.
   102→   102→- Depth unit.
   103→   103→- Layer identity.
   104→   104→- Sampling support.
   105→   105→- Measurement type.
   106→   106→- Source.
   107→   107→- Validity period.
   108→   108→
   109→   109→Support common depth intervals without assuming that all datasets or laboratories use the same intervals.
   110→   110→
   111→   111→At minimum, the model should accommodate:
   112→   112→
   113→   113→- 0–5 cm.
   114→   114→- 5–15 cm.
   115→   115→- 15–30 cm.
   116→   116→- 30–60 cm.
   117→   117→- 60–100 cm.
   118→   118→- 100–200 cm.
   119→   119→
   120→   120→These intervals are relevant to SoilGrids and GlobalSoilMap conventions, but field laboratory sampling must preserve its actual sampled depths.
   121→   121→
   122→   122→Do not silently convert a 0–5 cm sample into a 0–30 cm profile estimate.
   123→   123→
   124→   124→## 4.2 Soil sampling events
   125→   125→
   126→   126→Represent sampling as a first-class domain object.
   127→   127→
   128→   128→A sampling event should support:
   129→   129→
   130→   130→- Field ID.
   131→   131→- Sampling date.
   132→   132→- Sampling depth.
   133→   133→- Sampling method.
   134→   134→- Sampling purpose.
   135→   135→- Sampling locations.
   136→   136→- GPS coordinates.
   137→   137→- Management zone.
   138→   138→- Composite versus individual sample.
   139→   139→- Number of subsamples when recorded.
   140→   140→- Sampling equipment.
   141→   141→- Sample handling.
   142→   142→- Sample preparation.
   143→   143→- Sampling organization or person.
   144→   144→- Notes and photographs.
   145→   145→- Quality status.
   146→   146→
   147→   147→Support both:
   148→   148→
   149→   149→- Composite soil samples.
   150→   150→- Individual soil cores or measurements.
   151→   151→
   152→   152→Preserve relationships between a composite sample and its constituent subsamples.
   153→   153→
   154→   154→Do not assume all sampling points represent the field equally.
   155→   155→
   156→   156→## 4.3 Laboratory analysis metadata
   157→   157→
   158→   158→Every soil-test result must retain:
   159→   159→
   160→   160→- Laboratory.
   161→   161→- Analytical method.
   162→   162→- Extraction method.
   163→   163→- Extractant.
   164→   164→- Soil-to-solution ratio.
   165→   165→- Measurement temperature where relevant.
   166→   166→- Reporting basis.
   167→   167→- Detection limit.
   168→   168→- Quantification limit.
   169→   169→- Result qualifier.
   170→   170→- Measurement uncertainty when available.
   171→   171→- Quality-control status.
   172→   172→- Report reference.
   173→   173→
   174→   174→Different laboratory methods must remain distinguishable.
   175→   175→
   176→   176→Do not compare analytically different measurements as though they were identical.
   177→   177→
   178→   178→## 4.4 Soil Sample Import
   179→   179→
   180→   180→Build an extensible soil-laboratory CSV import workflow.
   181→   181→
   182→   182→Support:
   183→   183→
   184→   184→- Field validation.
   185→   185→- Sample identification.
   186→   186→- Depth validation.
   187→   187→- Soil property mapping.
   188→   188→- Unit normalization.
   189→   189→- Analytical method mapping.
   190→   190→- Detection-limit parsing.
   191→   191→- Missing-value handling.
   192→   192→- Duplicate detection.
   193→   193→- Row-level provenance.
   194→   194→- File checksum.
   195→   195→- Idempotency.
   196→   196→- Import warnings.
   197→   197→- Rejected-row reporting.
   198→   198→
   199→   199→Design the contract so different laboratory templates can be mapped into a common canonical schema.
   200→   200→
   201→   201→PDF and image report extraction may be considered a future extension, but never silently accept unverified OCR results as validated laboratory measurements.

   203→   203→# 5. M6B.2 — SOIL PHYSICAL INTELLIGENCE
   204→   204→
   205→   205→Develop a physical soil properties knowledge and measurement system.
   206→   206→
   207→   207→Support the following indicators when evidence exists:
   208→   208→
   209→   209→## Texture
   210→   210→
   211→   211→- Sand percentage.
   212→   212→- Silt percentage.
   213→   213→- Clay percentage.
   214→   214→- Soil texture classification.
   215→   215→- Classification system used.
   216→   216→- Texture measurement method.
   217→   217→
   218→   218→Ensure compatible fractions sum to 100% within defined numerical tolerance.
   219→   219→
   220→   220→Support USDA and other documented classification systems without mixing their classification boundaries.
   221→   221→
   222→   222→## Bulk density
   223→   223→
   224→   224→Represent:
   225→   225→
   226→   226→- Whole-soil bulk density.
   227→   227→- Fine-earth bulk density.
   228→   228→- Coarse fragment content.
   229→   229→- Sample depth.
   230→   230→- Measurement method.
   231→   231→- Unit.
   232→   232→- Measurement basis.
   233→   233→
   234→   234→Do not assume that bulk density is available merely because SoilGrids provides a prediction.
   235→   235→
   236→   236→## Porosity
   237→   237→
   238→   238→Where inputs support estimation, calculate total porosity using:
   239→   239→
   240→   240→n = 1 - BD / PD
   241→   241→
   242→   242→Where:
   243→   243→
   244→   244→- n is total porosity.
   245→   245→- BD is bulk density.
   246→   246→- PD is particle density.
   247→   247→
   248→   248→Never assume particle density without a documented value or justified assumption.
   249→   249→
   250→   250→Label calculated porosity as estimated when particle density is inferred.
   251→   251→
   252→   252→## Infiltration
   253→   253→
   254→   254→Support:
   255→   255→
   256→   256→- Infiltration rate.
   257→   257→- Measurement method.
   258→   258→- Initial moisture conditions.
   259→   259→- Surface conditions.
   260→   260→- Measurement duration.
   261→   261→- Unit.
   262→   262→- Repeated measurements.
   263→   263→
   264→   264→Do not compare infiltration values from incompatible procedures without a valid normalization or comparison methodology.
   265→   265→
   266→   266→## Aggregate stability
   267→   267→
   268→   268→Support the distinction between:
   269→   269→
   270→   270→- Wet aggregate stability.
   271→   271→- Dry aggregate stability.
   272→   272→- Mean weight diameter.
   273→   273→- Aggregate size distribution.
   274→   274→
   275→   275→Store the exact analytical procedure.
   276→   276→
   277→   277→## Compaction
   278→   278→
   279→   279→Support:
   280→   280→
   281→   281→- Penetration resistance.
   282→   282→- Measurement depth.
   283→   283→- Cone specification.
   284→   284→- Soil moisture conditions.
   285→   285→- Sampling method.
   286→   286→
   287→   287→Do not interpret penetration resistance independently of soil water conditions.
   288→   288→
   289→   289→## Available Water Capacity
   290→   290→
   291→   291→Store and evaluate:
   292→   292→
   293→   293→- Water content at field capacity.
   294→   294→- Water content at permanent wilting point.
   295→   295→- Water content measurement basis.
   296→   296→- Matric potential.
   297→   297→- Soil layer depth.
   298→   298→- Hydraulic measurement method.
   299→   299→
   300→   300→Keep these properties compatible with the existing M6A water-balance domain.
   301→   301→
   302→   302→Do not build a separate, inconsistent implementation of soil-water retention.
   303→   303→
   304→   304→# 6. M6B.3 — SOIL CHEMICAL INTELLIGENCE
   305→   305→
   306→   306→Support a method-aware chemical soil testing framework.
   307→   307→
   308→   308→## Core soil properties
   309→   309→
   310→   310→- pH.
   311→   311→- Electrical conductivity.
   312→   312→- Organic carbon.
   313→   313→- Total nitrogen.
   314→   314→- Available nitrogen.
   315→   315→- Available phosphorus.
   316→   316→- Extractable potassium.
   317→   317→- Cation exchange capacity.
   318→   318→- Exchangeable cations when available.
   319→   319→- Carbonate content when relevant.
   320→   320→- Sodium-related indicators where appropriate.
   321→   321→
   322→   322→## 6.1 Soil pH
   323→   323→
   324→   324→Always store:
   325→   325→
   326→   326→- pH value.
   327→   327→- Extraction medium.
   328→   328→- Soil-to-solution ratio.
   329→   329→- Analytical method.
   330→   330→- Sample depth.
   331→   331→- Laboratory.
   332→   332→
   333→   333→Distinguish pH measured in water, calcium chloride, and other extraction media.
   334→   334→
   335→   335→Do not apply a universal crop suitability threshold without considering measurement method, crop, soil, and region.
   336→   336→
   337→   337→## 6.2 Soil salinity
   338→   338→
   339→   339→Support:
   340→   340→
   341→   341→- ECe from saturated paste extract.
   342→   342→- EC measurements at other soil-to-water ratios.
   343→   343→- EC measurement temperature.
   344→   344→- Conversion methodology.
   345→   345→- Soil texture.
   346→   346→- Relevant calibration information.
   347→   347→
   348→   348→Do not equate EC 1:5 with ECe.
   349→   349→
   350→   350→Do not apply a generic conversion across all soil textures.
   351→   351→
   352→   352→## 6.3 Nutrient measurements
   353→   353→
   354→   354→Distinguish:
   355→   355→
   356→   356→- Total nutrient content.
   357→   357→- Extractable nutrient content.
   358→   358→- Plant-available nutrient estimates.
   359→   359→- Nutrient concentration.
   360→   360→- Nutrient stocks.
   361→   361→- Fertilizer application amounts.
   362→   362→
   363→   363→For phosphorus and potassium, preserve chemical reporting forms and convert oxide units to elemental forms only through explicit, versioned conversion functions.
   364→   364→
   365→   365→Do not treat total soil phosphorus as plant-available phosphorus.
   366→   366→
   367→   367→Do not interpret a nutrient concentration as a nutrient stock without the required physical soil parameters.
   368→   368→
   369→   369→# 7. M6B.4 — SOIL ORGANIC CARBON INTELLIGENCE
   370→   370→
   371→   371→This must be a scientifically rigorous, independently tested module.
   372→   372→
   373→   373→## 7.1 Distinguish carbon variables
   374→   374→
   375→   375→Keep these separate:
   376→   376→
   377→   377→- SOC concentration.
   378→   378→- Organic matter concentration.
   379→   379→- SOC density.
   380→   380→- SOC stock.
   381→   381→- SOC stock by depth.
   382→   382→- SOC stock change.
   383→   383→- Carbon inputs.
   384→   384→- Carbon removals.
   385→   385→- Estimated carbon sequestration.
   386→   386→
   387→   387→Organic matter and organic carbon are not identical.
   388→   388→
   389→   389→Do not use a universal organic matter-to-carbon conversion without declaring the method and its applicability.
   390→   390→
   391→   391→## 7.2 SOC stock calculation
   392→   392→
   393→   393→Implement a documented calculation based on appropriate soil measurements.
   394→   394→
   395→   395→Where inputs use the following basis:
   396→   396→
   397→   397→- OC: mg C/g fine earth.
   398→   398→- BDfine: g fine earth/cm³ fine earth.
   399→   399→- vCF: volumetric coarse-fragment fraction.
   400→   400→- d: layer thickness in cm.
   401→   401→
   402→   402→The FAO/IPCC-style equation is:
   403→   403→
   404→   404→SOC stock (Mg C/ha) =
   405→   405→OC × BDfine × (1 - vCF) × d × 0.1
   406→   406→
   407→   407→Verify dimensional consistency and independently test the implementation.
   408→   408→
   409→   409→If the bulk-density definition or coarse-fragment basis differs, use the appropriate documented alternative rather than applying this formula mechanically.
   410→   410→
   411→   411→Do not double-correct coarse fragments.
   412→   412→
   413→   413→Do not compute SOC stock where the required depth, density, concentration, or coarse-fragment information is missing.
   414→   414→
   415→   415→In those cases, show available concentration data and mark stock as not evaluated.
   416→   416→
   417→   417→## 7.3 Layer-wise stock calculation
   418→   418→
   419→   419→Calculate SOC separately for each layer.
   420→   420→
   421→   421→The total stock of a profile may be calculated as the sum of compatible layer stocks:
   422→   422→
   423→   423→SOC_profile = Σ SOC_layer
   424→   424→
   425→   425→Do not add overlapping or duplicated depth intervals.
   426→   426→
   427→   427→Validate gaps in the soil profile.
   428→   428→
   429→   429→## 7.4 Fixed-depth versus Equivalent Soil Mass
   430→   430→
   431→   431→Implement method-aware comparisons.
   432→   432→
   433→   433→Support:
   434→   434→
   435→   435→- Fixed-depth comparison.
   436→   436→- Equivalent soil mass comparison when required data exist.
   437→   437→
   438→   438→Explain that bulk-density changes can affect fixed-depth stock comparisons.
   439→   439→
   440→   440→Do not claim equivalent-soil-mass correction when additional sample depths or mass information are unavailable.
   441→   441→
   442→   442→Preserve the comparison method and version in every result.
   443→   443→
   444→   444→## 7.5 SoilGrids SOC integration
   445→   445→
   446→   446→Use existing SoilGrids provider contracts.
   447→   447→
   448→   448→Preserve:
   449→   449→
   450→   450→- Native pixel resolution.
   451→   451→- Depth intervals.
   452→   452→- Mean.
   453→   453→- Median.
   454→   454→- Q0.05.
   455→   455→- Q0.95.
   456→   456→- Spatial support.
   457→   457→- Product version.
   458→   458→- Prediction uncertainty.
   459→   459→
   460→   460→Important:
   461→   461→
   462→   462→Pixel-level Q0.05 and Q0.95 layers cannot simply be averaged and relabeled as a field-level 90% prediction interval.
   463→   463→
   464→   464→Implement an appropriate field-level uncertainty methodology, or clearly report the uncertainty aggregation as unavailable.
   465→   465→
   466→   466→Distinguish mapped estimates from field-measured soil samples.
   467→   467→
   468→   468→## 7.6 SOC trend analysis
   469→   469→
   470→   470→Compare SOC stocks only when:
   471→   471→
   472→   472→- Depth intervals are compatible.
   473→   473→- Sampling methods are sufficiently comparable.
   474→   474→- Bulk-density methodology is available.
   475→   475→- Coarse-fragment treatment is compatible.
   476→   476→- Sampling locations and spatial support are documented.
   477→   477→- Analytical methods are comparable.
   478→   478→
   479→   479→Expose differences in methodology before showing temporal trends.
   480→   480→
   481→   481→# 8. M6B.5 — NUTRIENT CYCLING AND BUDGET ENGINE
   482→   482→
   483→   483→Build a season-aware nutrient accounting system for N, P, and K.
   484→   484→
   485→   485→## 8.1 Nutrient input records
   486→   486→
   487→   487→Support:
   488→   488→
   489→   489→- Synthetic fertilizers.
   490→   490→- Manure.
   491→   491→- Compost.
   492→   492→- Organic amendments.
   493→   493→- Biological nitrogen fixation where estimated using documented methods.
   494→   494→- Atmospheric nitrogen deposition where supported.
   495→   495→- Nutrients in irrigation water when measured.
   496→   496→- Other documented nutrient inputs.
   497→   497→
   498→   498→Each input must support:
   499→   499→
   500→   500→- Application date.
   501→   501→- Applied mass.
   502→   502→- Application area.
   503→   503→- Product identity.
   504→   504→- Nutrient grade.
   505→   505→- Nutrient concentration.
   506→   506→- Moisture basis.
   507→   507→- Available fraction when scientifically justified.
   508→   508→- Application method.
   509→   509→- Reference and provenance.
   510→   510→
   511→   511→## 8.2 Nutrient output records
   512→   512→
   513→   513→Support:
   514→   514→
   515→   515→- Harvested crop products.
   516→   516→- Crop residues removed.
   517→   517→- Other biomass exported.
   518→   518→
   519→   519→Calculate nutrient removal using:
   520→   520→
   521→   521→Exported biomass × nutrient concentration
   522→   522→
   523→   523→Maintain explicit dry-matter versus as-harvest basis.
   524→   524→
   525→   525→Do not infer nutrient removal using yield alone without a sourced concentration or defensible estimate.
   526→   526→
   527→   527→## 8.3 Nutrient budget
   528→   528→
   529→   529→Implement a clearly defined partial or full nutrient budget.
   530→   530→
   531→   531→General principle:
   532→   532→
   533→   533→Nutrient balance = Total documented inputs - Total documented outputs
   534→   534→
   535→   535→Where reliable information exists, include:
   536→   536→
   537→   537→- Fertilizer inputs.
   538→   538→- Manure inputs.
   539→   539→- Biological nitrogen fixation.
   540→   540→- Atmospheric N deposition.
   541→   541→- Irrigation nutrient inputs.
   542→   542→- Harvest removal.
   543→   543→- Residue removal.
   544→   544→- Relevant nutrient losses.
   545→   545→
   546→   546→Crucial restrictions:
   547→   547→
   548→   548→1. Do not silently treat unmeasured leaching as zero.
   549→   549→2. Do not treat unknown denitrification or volatilization as zero.
   550→   550→3. Do not assume all manure nutrients are immediately plant-available.
   551→   551→4. Do not confuse nutrient application with nutrient uptake.
   552→   552→5. Do not interpret a partial balance as a full soil nutrient stock change.
   553→   553→6. Do not calculate nitrogen fixation from legume classification alone.
   554→   554→
   555→   555→Return:
   556→   556→
   557→   557→- Complete balance.
   558→   558→- Partial balance.
   559→   559→- Not evaluated.
   560→   560→
   561→   561→Explain which flows are missing.
   562→   562→
   563→   563→## 8.4 Nutrient use efficiency
   564→   564→
   565→   565→If implemented, every NUE definition must be explicit.
   566→   566→
   567→   567→For example, distinguish nutrient output/input ratios from agronomic efficiency and apparent recovery efficiency.
   568→   568→
   569→   569→Do not present one efficiency calculation as universally applicable to all nutrient management systems.
   570→   570→
   571→   571→## 8.5 Rotation-level nutrient effects
   572→   572→
   573→   573→Integrate the nutrient model with crop rotation history.
   574→   574→
   575→   575→Consider:
   576→   576→
   577→   577→- Nutrient removal by crop.
   578→   578→- Residue retention or removal.
   579→   579→- Manure and fertilizer inputs.
   580→   580→- Legume-related N inputs when documented.
   581→   581→- Nutrient balance by season.
   582→   582→- Cumulative nutrient balance.
   583→   583→- Nutrient management implications.
   584→   584→
   585→   585→Do not automatically assign nitrogen credits to all legume crops.
   586→   586→

   603→   603→
   604→   604→- Sample handling.
   605→   605→- Incubation duration.
   606→   606→- Temperature.
   607→   607→- Analytical protocol.
   608→   608→- Unit.
   609→   609→- Reference.
   610→   610→- Sampling date.
   611→   611→
   612→   612→Do not simulate actual microbial activity from generic soil characteristics without a validated model.
   613→   613→
   614→   614→Do not assume that missing biological measurements imply poor soil health.
   615→   615→
   616→   616→# 10. M6B.7 — MULTI-DIMENSIONAL SOIL HEALTH ASSESSMENT
   617→   617→
   618→   618→Build a multi-domain assessment rather than an opaque universal score.
   619→   619→
   620→   620→The main domains should be:
   621→   621→
   622→   622→1. Physical.
   623→   623→2. Chemical.
   624→   624→3. Biological.
   625→   625→4. Hydrological.
   626→   626→5. Organic matter and nutrient cycling.
   627→   627→
   628→   628→For each domain, display:
   629→   629→
   630→   630→- Available indicators.
   631→   631→- Missing indicators.
   632→   632→- Measurement type.
   633→   633→- Historical trend.
   634→   634→- Data quality.
   635→   635→- Reference methodology.
   636→   636→- Local applicability.
   637→   637→- Uncertainty.
   638→   638→- Interpretation.
   639→   639→
   640→   640→Do not make an unsupported overall claim such as "excellent soil health".
   641→   641→
   642→   642→A composite index may be introduced only if a defensible, validated methodology exists.
   643→   643→
   644→   644→If a composite index is used, disclose:
   645→   645→
   646→   646→- Indicator definitions.
   647→   647→- Normalization.
   648→   648→- Weighting.
   649→   649→- Missing-data handling.
   650→   650→- Regional applicability.
   651→   651→- Sensitivity to weights.
   652→   652→- Validation status.
   653→   653→
   654→   654→The default output should be a transparent profile of soil functions.

   670→   670→- Irrigation.
   671→   671→- Soil amendments.
