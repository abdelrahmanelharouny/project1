    11→    11→
    12→    12→# 0. EXECUTIVE DIRECTIVE
    13→    13→
    14→    14→Build a practical, scientifically defensible **Field Management Intelligence** capability that converts crop-rotation plans, soil and water information, crop knowledge, field history, and farmer constraints into an explainable seasonal management plan.
    15→    15→
    16→    16→M6C is not a generic farm ERP, a static crop calendar, or a simplistic cover-crop recommender. It must help a farmer or agronomist answer:
    17→    17→
    18→    18→- When is the field likely to be without protective soil cover?
    19→    19→- Which management options could reduce that exposed period without creating unacceptable water, timing, pest, or operational trade-offs?
    20→    20→- What crop residues are available, how much is retained or removed, and what evidence supports those quantities?
    21→    21→- Which cover-crop candidates fit the local planting and termination windows, the following cash crop, water availability, equipment, and market or livestock needs?
    22→    22→- What field operations are required, in what sequence, and with which machinery and labor resources?
    23→    23→- Which operations conflict with one another or with crop, soil-moisture, weather, or field-access constraints?
    24→    24→- What erosion processes can be screened with the available evidence, and which cannot be assessed?
    25→    25→- Why was a practice suggested, excluded, or left unevaluated?
    26→    26→
    27→    27→The key deliverable is an **explainable field management plan** connected to the existing rotation, water, soil, knowledge, and provenance systems.
    28→    28→
    29→    29→## Non-negotiable principles
    30→    30→
    31→    31→1. Reuse existing M1–M6B contracts and domain models; do not duplicate them.
    32→    32→2. Distinguish measured, farmer-reported, mapped, modeled, reference, expert-reviewed, and synthetic evidence.
    33→    33→3. Do not invent crop calendars, seeding rates, termination dates, residue coefficients, erosion factors, machinery capacities, labor availability, or weather forecasts.
    34→    34→4. Do not claim that a practice caused a measured soil or yield change from temporal association alone.
    35→    35→5. Do not treat a cover crop as universally beneficial: it can protect soil and recycle nutrients while also consuming water and competing with the following crop.
    36→    36→6. Do not turn a screening indicator into a quantitative prediction unless the selected methodology and inputs support it.
    37→    37→7. Do not silently relax operational or agronomic hard constraints.
    38→    38→8. Preserve source resolution and spatial limitations.
    39→    39→9. Every calculation and schedule must be reproducible and versioned.
    40→    40→10. The system must be allowed to return `not_evaluated`, `conditional`, `infeasible`, or `insufficient_evidence` rather than forcing a recommendation.

    85→    85→# 2. M6C DOMAIN MODEL AND BOUNDARIES
    86→    86→
    87→    87→M6C should contain the following cohesive capabilities. Keep them as modules within the existing architecture, not separate microservices.
    88→    88→
    89→    89→## 2.1 Management Practice Knowledge
    90→    90→
    91→    91→A versioned registry of practices and their requirements, intended mechanisms, evidence, risks, and regional applicability.
    92→    92→
    93→    93→Examples:
    94→    94→
    95→    95→- Cover cropping.
    96→    96→- Surface residue retention.
    97→    97→- Residue removal or baling.
    98→    98→- Residue incorporation.
    99→    99→- Mulching.
   100→   100→- Reduced tillage / strip tillage / no-till where locally appropriate.
   101→   101→- Contour operations.
   102→   102→- Strip cropping.
   103→   103→- Buffer strips or vegetated barriers.
   104→   104→- Controlled traffic where supported.
   105→   105→- Cover-crop termination methods.
   106→   106→- Field access and traffic management.
   107→   107→
   108→   108→Do not represent these as universally beneficial switches. Each practice requires context, operating conditions, and trade-offs.
   109→   109→
   110→   110→## 2.2 Soil Cover and Bare-Soil Window Intelligence
   111→   111→
   112→   112→Represent field soil-cover status over time using available evidence:
   113→   113→
   114→   114→- Living crop canopy.
   115→   115→- Living cover crop.
   116→   116→- Surface crop residue.
   117→   117→- Mulch or other protective cover.
   118→   118→- Bare or insufficiently observed surface.
   119→   119→- Unknown cover status.
   120→   120→
   121→   121→Support both observed and modeled cover timelines. Preserve the method and evidence class. Do not infer measured percentage cover from NDVI without a validated, regionally applicable conversion model.
   122→   122→
   123→   123→## 2.3 Cover-Crop Planning
   124→   124→
   125→   125→Generate context-specific cover-crop candidates and compare their potential functions, establishment requirements, water use, termination needs, and compatibility with the next main crop.
   126→   126→
   127→   127→## 2.4 Crop Residue and Biomass Accounting
   128→   128→
   129→   129→Track residue production, harvest removal, collection, retention, incorporation, grazing, burning where legally relevant, and other documented fates. Keep dry-matter basis and nutrient/carbon content explicit.
   130→   130→
   131→   131→## 2.5 Erosion and Conservation Screening
   132→   132→
   133→   133→Screen relevant erosion hazards and evaluate management alternatives only within the domain supported by the selected model and evidence. Separate sheet/rill erosion, concentrated-flow/gully processes, wind erosion, streambank erosion, and sediment delivery.
   134→   134→
   135→   135→## 2.6 Field Operations Planning
   136→   136→
   137→   137→Convert rotation and management plans into tasks with windows, durations, precedence, machinery, labor, access, and resource constraints. Identify conflicts and unscheduled work transparently.
   138→   138→
   139→   139→## 2.7 Practice Trade-off Evaluation
   140→   140→
   141→   141→Compare practices across soil cover, erosion protection, water demand, nutrient cycling, crop establishment, pests/diseases, labor, machinery, costs, and market/livestock value where data exists.
   142→   142→
   143→   143→## 2.8 Farmer Action Plan
   144→   144→
   145→   145→Present a seasonal, editable and trackable calendar of operations, deadlines, dependencies, alerts, and reasons. Allow farmer and agronomist feedback.
   146→   146→

   149→   149→# 3. KNOWLEDGE AND EVIDENCE MODEL
   150→   150→
   151→   151→Every practice, cover crop trait, residue coefficient, operation requirement, and erosion parameter must be evidence-backed or explicitly unknown.
   152→   152→
   153→   153→## 3.1 Required knowledge attributes
   154→   154→
   155→   155→A knowledge record should support:
   156→   156→
   157→   157→- `knowledge_code`
   158→   158→- `entity_type` and `entity_id`
   159→   159→- `attribute_code`
   160→   160→- `value` and `unit`
   161→   161→- `source_id` / `reference_id`
   162→   162→- publication or dataset version
   163→   163→- exact source location (page, table, section, formula, or dataset field when available)
   164→   164→- geographic applicability (country, region, agroecological zone, or geometry)
   165→   165→- soil/climate conditions
   166→   166→- crop species and cultivar applicability
   167→   167→- growth stage or operation stage
   168→   168→- validity interval
   169→   169→- evidence class
   170→   170→- uncertainty description or interval when supplied by the source
   171→   171→- review status
   172→   172→- license and attribution
   173→   173→
   174→   174→## 3.2 Evidence classes
   175→   175→
   176→   176→Preserve distinctions among:
   177→   177→
   178→   178→- `field_measured`
   179→   179→- `laboratory_measured`
   180→   180→- `farmer_reported`
   181→   181→- `remote_sensing_estimate`
   182→   182→- `reanalysis_estimate`
   183→   183→- `model_estimate`
   184→   184→- `published_reference`
   185→   185→- `expert_reviewed_reference`
   186→   186→- `confirmed_regional`
   187→   187→- `synthetic_test_data`
   188→   188→
   189→   189→A `published_reference` is not automatically `confirmed_regional`. A model estimate is not a field measurement. Synthetic data must never contribute to real-farm recommendations.
   190→   190→
   191→   191→## 3.3 Regional applicability
   192→   192→
   193→   193→Support regional knowledge packs with explicit coverage and versioning. Do not infer a regional recommendation from latitude or hemisphere alone. Crop seasons and cover-crop windows may depend on monsoon, irrigation, bimodal rainfall, elevation, local cultivar, and management system.
   194→   194→
   195→   195→## 3.4 Conflicting evidence
   196→   196→
   197→   197→If reputable sources disagree, store both claims and their context. Do not silently average incompatible recommendations. Provide an evidence conflict state and require expert review where the disagreement can change a decision.
   198→   198→

   201→   201→# 4. COVER-CROP PLANNING ENGINE
   202→   202→
   203→   203→This is a core M6C capability, but it must be a transparent candidate and planning engine, not a universal species ranking.
   204→   204→
   205→   205→## 4.1 Cover-crop functional groups
   206→   206→
   207→   207→Represent functions as evidence-backed traits, not fixed assumptions:
   208→   208→
   209→   209→- Grasses / cereals: potential rapid ground cover, fibrous roots, nutrient capture, and residue production.
   210→   210→- Legumes: potential biological nitrogen fixation, subject to host compatibility, inoculation, biomass, growth, and management conditions.
   211→   211→- Brassicas and other broadleaf species: possible rooting, nutrient scavenging, biofumigation or other functions only where crop-specific evidence supports the claim.
   212→   212→- Mixtures: composition, seeding proportion, competitive interactions, and management complexity must be represented.
   213→   213→
   214→   214→Do not infer function solely from family membership.
   215→   215→
   216→   216→## 4.2 Candidate eligibility
   217→   217→
   218→   218→For each field and between-crop window, evaluate:
   219→   219→
   220→   220→- Locally valid sowing window.
   221→   221→- Time available for establishment and biomass accumulation.
   222→   222→- Crop duration and growth-stage development.
   223→   223→- Temperature and photoperiod requirements where documented.
   224→   224→- Frost or heat constraints where applicable.
   225→   225→- Soil texture, pH, salinity, drainage, and other relevant soil properties.
   226→   226→- Water availability and M6A demand estimates.
   227→   227→- Following cash-crop planting date.
   228→   228→- Termination method and equipment availability.
   229→   229→- Potential pest, disease, weed, or allelopathy concerns.
   230→   230→- Seed availability and local legality/invasiveness constraints where known.
   231→   231→- Livestock/forage value and grazing constraints if requested.
   232→   232→- Farmer preferences and budget.
   233→   233→
   234→   234→Return candidate classes:
   235→   235→
   236→   236→- `eligible`
   237→   237→- `conditional`
   238→   238→- `excluded`
   239→   239→- `not_evaluated`
   240→   240→
   241→   241→Every condition and exclusion must be explained. Missing evidence must not become eligibility.
   242→   242→
   243→   243→## 4.3 Water competition and termination
   244→   244→
   245→   245→Integrate with M6A, not a parallel water model.
   246→   246→
   247→   247→Evaluate potential cover-crop water use and the following crop's establishment risk only when appropriate Kc, crop-stage, water-balance, and calendar inputs are available.
   248→   248→
   249→   249→Termination must be represented as a decision with:
   250→   250→
   251→   251→- Target termination window.
   252→   252→- Method.
   253→   253→- Required machinery or labor.
   254→   254→- Time needed before next crop operations.
   255→   255→- Water implications.
   256→   256→- Biomass/residue implications.
   257→   257→- Regrowth or seed-set risk where relevant.
   258→   258→- Evidence and regional applicability.
   259→   259→
   260→   260→Do not hardcode a universal termination lead time (for example, a fixed number of days before planting). Such timing is species-, climate-, soil-, method-, and crop-system-dependent.
   261→   261→
   262→   262→## 4.4 Cover-crop output
   263→   263→
   264→   264→For each candidate return:
   265→   265→
   266→   266→- Species / cultivar or mixture identity.
   267→   267→- Intended function(s).
   268→   268→- Eligibility status.
   269→   269→- Planting window and its evidence.
   270→   270→- Establishment requirements.
   271→   271→- Expected duration only if supported.
   272→   272→- Water-demand profile or explicit missing-data status.
   273→   273→- Termination options and constraints.
   274→   274→- Following-crop compatibility.
   275→   275→- Benefits, trade-offs, and risks.
   276→   276→- Knowledge coverage and missing inputs.
   277→   277→- References and version identifiers.
   278→   278→
   279→   279→Do not generate an agronomic recommendation when the minimum regional crop calendar or critical water/termination knowledge is absent. Return a research candidate or unevaluated option instead.
   280→   280→

   282→   282→
   283→   283→# 5. SOIL COVER AND BARE-SOIL WINDOW ENGINE
   284→   284→
   285→   285→## 5.1 Temporal representation
   286→   286→
   287→   287→Build an interval-based field cover timeline. Track:
   288→   288→
   289→   289→- Main crop establishment and harvest.
   290→   290→- Cover-crop establishment and termination.
   291→   291→- Residue deposition and removal.
   292→   292→- Mulch events.
   293→   293→- Tillage or disturbance events.
   294→   294→- Remote-sensing cover observations when available.
   295→   295→- Farmer-observed cover measurements.
   296→   296→- Unknown intervals.
   297→   297→
   298→   298→## 5.2 Coverage measures
   299→   299→
   300→   300→Calculate transparent metrics such as:
   301→   301→
   302→   302→- Number of days with known living cover.
   303→   303→- Number of days with known residue cover.
   304→   304→- Number of days with known bare surface.
   305→   305→- Unknown observation days.
   306→   306→- Longest observed uncovered interval.
   307→   307→- Fraction of the planning window with evidence of protective cover.
   308→   308→- Seasonal cover timeline.
   309→   309→
   310→   310→Do not label all missing observations as bare soil. Use `unknown` where the status is not observed or inferable.
   311→   311→
   312→   312→## 5.3 Cover thresholds
   313→   313→
   314→   314→Do not apply a universal “safe cover percentage” or minimum cover threshold to every region and conservation standard.
   315→   315→
   316→   316→Thresholds must be sourced, versioned, and scoped to the applicable practice standard or regional objective. If no threshold is applicable, report the actual cover metrics without pass/fail classification.
   317→   317→
   318→   318→## 5.4 Spatial cover
   319→   319→
   320→   320→If remote sensing is introduced or already available, distinguish:
   321→   321→
   322→   322→- Vegetation index.
   323→   323→- Estimated fractional vegetation cover.
   324→   324→- Measured ground cover.
   325→   325→- Residue cover.
   326→   326→- Total protective cover.
   327→   327→
   328→   328→NDVI alone cannot distinguish all forms of residue and green vegetation. Do not equate NDVI with percentage soil cover without a validated model and appropriate sensor/time conditions.
   329→   329→

   331→   331→
   332→   332→# 6. CROP RESIDUE AND BIOMASS MANAGEMENT ENGINE
   333→   333→
   334→   334→## 6.1 Residue mass accounting
   335→   335→
   336→   336→Represent residue flows on a dry-matter basis whenever possible:
   337→   337→
   338→   338→- Above-ground biomass produced.
   339→   339→- Harvested product.
   340→   340→- Residue generated.
   341→   341→- Residue retained on surface.
   342→   342→- Residue incorporated.
   343→   343→- Residue removed or baled.
   344→   344→- Residue grazed.
   345→   345→- Residue burned or otherwise lost where relevant and legally recorded.
   346→   346→- Unknown/unaccounted fraction.
   347→   347→
   348→   348→Use mass-balance checks. Never allow negative residue availability. Do not infer residue biomass from grain yield unless a crop-, cultivar-, environment-, and management-appropriate harvest-index or residue-to-product relationship is sourced and versioned.
   349→   349→
   350→   350→## 6.2 Residue properties
   351→   351→
   352→   352→Support where evidence exists:
   353→   353→
   354→   354→- Dry matter.
   355→   355→- Carbon fraction.
   356→   356→- Nitrogen concentration.
   357→   357→- C:N ratio.
   358→   358→- Lignin or quality indicators where relevant.
   359→   359→- Particle size and orientation if relevant to cover/erosion modeling.
   360→   360→- Decomposition method and conditions.
   361→   361→
   362→   362→Do not use one universal decomposition constant or C:N threshold across residues and climates.
   363→   363→
   364→   364→## 6.3 Residue management trade-offs
   365→   365→
   366→   366→Evaluate documented implications for:
   367→   367→
   368→   368→- Surface protection.
   369→   369→- Soil organic carbon inputs.
   370→   370→- Nutrient recycling.
   371→   371→- Water infiltration and evaporation.
   372→   372→- Seedbed and planting interference.
   373→   373→- Pest/disease carryover.
   374→   374→- Livestock feed value.
   375→   375→- Bioenergy or market value.
   376→   376→- Removal costs and machinery.
   377→   377→
   378→   378→Do not assume residue retention is always optimal. Balance soil protection and nutrient cycling against feed, fuel, market, and operational requirements. Surface cover benefits and removal risks must be interpreted in the field and regional context.
   379→   379→
   380→   380→## 6.4 Integrate with M6B
   381→   381→
   382→   382→Reuse M6B management events, nutrient budgets, and soil profile contracts. Record residue events so that subsequent nutrient accounting and soil-carbon analyses can consume them with provenance.
   383→   383→
   384→   384→A residue input is not automatically equivalent to stable soil organic carbon sequestration. Keep carbon input, decomposition, and SOC stock change conceptually separate.
   385→   385→

   387→   387→
   388→   388→# 7. EROSION AND CONSERVATION RISK INTELLIGENCE
   389→   389→
   390→   390→This area requires strict model applicability controls.
   391→   391→
   392→   392→## 7.1 Erosion process taxonomy
   393→   393→
   394→   394→Keep distinct:
   395→   395→
   396→   396→- Sheet/interrill erosion.
   397→   397→- Rill erosion.
   398→   398→- Ephemeral gully erosion.
   399→   399→- Classical gully erosion.
   400→   400→- Wind erosion.
   401→   401→- Streambank/channel erosion.
   402→   402→- Deposition and sediment delivery.
   403→   403→
   404→   404→A model designed for rainfall-driven sheet and rill erosion must not be presented as estimating all erosion processes.
   405→   405→
   406→   406→## 7.2 Terrain and hydrology inputs
   407→   407→
   408→   408→Potential inputs include:
   409→   409→
   410→   410→- Slope and aspect.
   411→   411→- Slope length / flow path.
   412→   412→- Curvature.
   413→   413→- Flow accumulation.
   414→   414→- Convergent areas.
   415→   415→- Soil erodibility properties.
   416→   416→- Rainfall erosivity.
   417→   417→- Infiltration/runoff evidence.
   418→   418→- Surface cover and residue.
   419→   419→- Crop sequence and tillage operations.
   420→   420→- Conservation support practices.
   421→   421→
   422→   422→Do not calculate slope length from a DEM using a naive straight-line distance. Flow routing, concentrated flow, terraces, and deposition boundaries matter.
   423→   423→
   424→   424→## 7.3 RUSLE/RUSLE2 adapter policy
   425→   425→
   426→   426→If RUSLE/RUSLE2 is implemented or integrated, treat it as a model-specific pathway for rainfall-driven sheet and rill erosion within its supported assumptions.
   427→   427→
   428→   428→The familiar USLE structure is:
   429→   429→
   430→   430→`A = R × K × LS × C × P`
   431→   431→
   432→   432→However:
   433→   433→
   434→   434→- Factor units and definitions must follow the selected RUSLE version and regional parameter set.
   435→   435→- Do not combine factor values from incompatible unit systems.
   436→   436→- Do not use US-specific NRCS factor databases as global defaults.
   437→   437→- Do not invent R, K, C, or P values.
   438→   438→- Do not assume DEM resolution guarantees accurate slope-length estimation.
   439→   439→- Do not label the result as watershed sediment yield.
   440→   440→- Do not claim coverage of ephemeral gullies, classical gullies, wind erosion, or channel erosion unless a separate validated model supports them.
   441→   441→
   442→   442→Where inputs or regional factors are missing, return `screening_only` or `not_evaluated`, with a clear reason.
   443→   443→
   444→   444→## 7.4 Erosion risk screening
   445→   445→
   446→   446→A qualitative screening pathway may identify evidence of concern from slope, soil, rainfall, cover, and management records. It must remain a screening result, not a numeric soil-loss prediction.
   447→   447→
   448→   448→Keep these outputs separate:
   449→   449→
   450→   450→- Evidence of erosion susceptibility.
   451→   451→- Model-estimated sheet/rill soil loss.
   452→   452→- Field-observed erosion signs.
   453→   453→- Unknown erosion status.
   454→   454→
   455→   455→## 7.5 Management alternatives
   456→   456→
   457→   457→Compare practices such as:
   458→   458→
   459→   459→- Maintain surface residues.
   460→   460→- Establish cover crops.
   461→   461→- Adjust tillage timing or intensity.
   462→   462→- Contour-aligned operations.
   463→   463→- Strip cropping.
   464→   464→- Vegetated barriers/buffer strips.
   465→   465→- Field traffic management.
   466→   466→
   467→   467→Only evaluate a practice quantitatively if the model has appropriate parameters and local applicability. Otherwise provide a sourced qualitative mechanism and identify local design requirements.
   468→   468→

   471→   471→# 8. FIELD OPERATIONS AND RESOURCE SCHEDULING ENGINE
   472→   472→
   473→   473→This is a practical differentiator: FieldShift should help turn a rotation plan into an executable sequence of farm operations.
   474→   474→
   475→   475→## 8.1 Operation templates
   476→   476→
   477→   477→Support configurable templates for relevant operations, for example:
   478→   478→
   479→   479→- Residue chopping or spreading.
   480→   480→- Tillage or seedbed preparation.
   481→   481→- Soil sampling.
   482→   482→- Cover-crop seeding.
   483→   483→- Main-crop planting.
   484→   484→- Fertilizer/manure application.
   485→   485→- Irrigation operations.
   486→   486→- Cover-crop termination.
   487→   487→- Spraying where legally and operationally relevant.
   488→   488→- Harvest.
   489→   489→- Residue baling/removal.
   490→   490→- Post-harvest field preparation.
   491→   491→
   492→   492→Operations must be derived from the actual crop/management plan and regional operation templates. Do not generate every possible operation for every crop.
   493→   493→
   494→   494→## 8.2 Operation data model
   495→   495→
   496→   496→Each operation should support:
   497→   497→
   498→   498→- Operation type.
   499→   499→- Field or management-zone scope.
   500→   500→- Related crop and season.
   501→   501→- Earliest start and latest finish.
   502→   502→- Preferred window.
   503→   503→- Duration or workload estimate.
   504→   504→- Area or quantity.
   505→   505→- Required machinery.
   506→   506→- Required implement.
   507→   507→- Labor skill/capacity.
   508→   508→- Fuel/material requirements where recorded.
   509→   509→- Predecessor and successor tasks.
   510→   510→- Soil condition restrictions.
   511→   511→- Weather restrictions.
   512→   512→- Access/trafficability restrictions.
   513→   513→- Completion state.
   514→   514→- Evidence and source of the operation template.
   515→   515→
   516→   516→Keep planned, scheduled, started, completed, delayed, cancelled, and blocked states distinct.
   517→   517→
   518→   518→## 8.3 Resource registry
   519→   519→
   520→   520→Support optional farmer-provided records for:
   521→   521→
   522→   522→- Tractors.
   523→   523→- Implements.
   524→   524→- Seeders.
   525→   525→- Sprayers.
   526→   526→- Harvesters.
   527→   527→- Balers.
   528→   528→- Irrigation equipment.
   529→   529→- Contractors.
   530→   530→- Labor crews.
   531→   531→- Storage and transport capacity.
   532→   532→
   533→   533→Include availability calendar, capacity, compatibility, working width, field efficiency where known, and maintenance/unavailability periods.
   534→   534→
   535→   535→Do not assume resource capacity or machine speed if not supplied or sourced.
   536→   536→
   537→   537→## 8.4 Scheduling model
   538→   538→
   539→   539→Use an appropriate scheduling formulation, potentially OR-Tools CP-SAT interval variables, only after inspecting existing optimization abstractions.
   540→   540→
   541→   541→Support:
   542→   542→
   543→   543→- Precedence constraints.
   544→   544→- Non-overlap constraints for exclusive resources.
   545→   545→- Resource capacity constraints.
   546→   546→- Operation windows.
   547→   547→- Labor availability.
   548→   548→- Machinery availability.
   549→   549→- Field access constraints.
   550→   550→- Setup/travel time where data exists.
   551→   551→- Weather and soil condition windows where actual forecasts/measurements and valid thresholds exist.
   552→   552→- Priority levels and farmer-selected objectives.
   553→   553→
   554→   554→Do not present a schedule as guaranteed executable when weather forecasts, machinery availability, or field conditions are unknown.
   555→   555→
   556→   556→## 8.5 Soil trafficability and compaction risk
   557→   557→
   558→   558→Only evaluate trafficability if the system has defensible inputs and a valid method, such as soil moisture/consistency, texture, machine axle load, tire pressure, and relevant field/soil parameters.
   559→   559→
   560→   560→Do not invent a universal soil-moisture threshold for machinery entry.
   561→   561→
   562→   562→When the required inputs are missing, display a manual field-readiness check rather than an automatic pass.
   563→   563→
   564→   564→## 8.6 Weather windows
   565→   565→
   566→   566→Historical climate data is not a weather forecast.
   567→   567→
   568→   568→Do not use climate normals as if they were next-week forecasts.
   569→   569→
   570→   570→Allow forecast information only through an explicit forecast provider or farmer-entered forecast. Record forecast issue time, valid time, provider, uncertainty, and expiration.
   571→   571→
   572→   572→If no forecast exists, schedule within agronomic date windows and clearly label weather checks as pending.
   573→   573→
   574→   574→## 8.7 Schedule explanations
   575→   575→
   576→   576→For every unscheduled or delayed task, explain the cause:
   577→   577→
   578→   578→- No available machine.
   579→   579→- Labor conflict.
   580→   580→- Predecessor incomplete.
   581→   581→- Outside sowing/harvest window.
   582→   582→- Water allocation conflict.
   583→   583→- Weather information missing.
   584→   584→- Soil trafficability unevaluated.
   585→   585→- Required agronomic knowledge missing.
   586→   586→
   587→   587→Never silently drop a task to produce a visually clean calendar.

   591→   591→# 9. PRACTICE TRADE-OFF AND DECISION ENGINE
   592→   592→
   593→   593→Compare management alternatives across multiple dimensions:
   594→   594→
   595→   595→- Soil cover duration.
   596→   596→- Surface residue availability.
   597→   597→- Erosion susceptibility or modelled sheet/rill soil loss, if supported.
   598→   598→- Water demand and following-crop water risk.
   599→   599→- Nutrient cycling.
   600→   600→- Soil organic matter input potential.
   601→   601→- Crop establishment and termination risk.
   602→   602→- Pest/disease concerns.
   603→   603→- Labor and machinery demand.
   604→   604→- Cost and revenue where evidence exists.
   605→   605→- Forage/livestock value where relevant.
   606→   606→
   607→   607→Separate hard constraints from soft objectives.
   608→   608→
   609→   609→Generate candidate plans such as:
   610→   610→
   611→   611→- Baseline farmer practice.
   612→   612→- Residue-retention-oriented plan.
   613→   613→- Cover-crop-oriented plan.
   614→   614→- Water-conserving cover/termination plan.
   615→   615→- Erosion-control-oriented plan.
   616→   616→- Balanced management plan.
   617→   617→
   618→   618→Do not preselect a plan as globally best. Use Pareto-aware comparison and display trade-offs. Preserve unevaluated criteria and distinguish evidence coverage from outcome confidence.
